# Bridgham, Carroll & Thornton 2006 — Evolution of hormone-receptor complexity by molecular exploitation

## Citation

Bridgham JT, Carroll SM, Thornton JW. **Evolution of hormone-receptor complexity by molecular exploitation.** *Science* 312(5770):97–101, 7 April 2006. doi:[10.1126/science.1123348](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1123348). PMID: 16601189.

## Abstract (verbatim)

> According to Darwinian theory, complexity evolves by a stepwise process of elaboration and optimization under natural selection. Biological systems composed of tightly integrated parts seem to challenge this view, because it is not obvious how any element's function can be selected for unless the partners with which it interacts are already present. Here we demonstrate how an integrated molecular system — the specific functional interaction between the steroid hormone aldosterone and its partner the mineralocorticoid receptor — evolved by a stepwise Darwinian process. Using ancestral gene resurrection, we show that, long before the hormone evolved, the receptor's affinity for aldosterone was present as a structural by-product of its partnership with chemically similar, more ancient ligands. Introducing two amino acid changes into the ancestral sequence recapitulates the evolution of present-day receptor specificity. Our results indicate that tight interactions can evolve by molecular exploitation — recruitment of an older molecule, previously constrained for a different role, into a new functional complex.

## What the paper supports

- An **ancestral corticoid receptor (AncCR)** existed before the MR/GR gene duplication, reconstructed via ancestral gene resurrection.
- AncCR already had binding affinity for aldosterone as a **structural by-product** of its partnership with older, chemically similar ligands (i.e. affinity predated the ligand).
- **Two amino-acid substitutions** in the ancestral sequence are sufficient to recapitulate the evolution of present-day receptor specificity.
- The paper names and defends the mechanism: **molecular exploitation** — a pre-existing molecule, constrained for a different role, is recruited into a new functional complex.

## What the paper does NOT establish (from the abstract alone)

- **The date of the MR/GR duplication.** The abstract does not give a numerical age or the lineage (e.g., "jawed vertebrates", "~450 Mya"). That claim, common in secondary sources, rests on related work (e.g. Thornton 2001 on vertebrate steroid receptor phylogeny), not this paper.
- **The GR side of the split.** The molecular-exploitation case made here is the MR/aldosterone partnership. The page's earlier phrasing "GR specialized for glucocorticoid signaling via two mutations" conflates the two lineages; the two-mutation result in the abstract is about the receptor–aldosterone specificity, i.e. the MR lineage.
- **Physiological consequences.** This is a molecular-evolution paper; claims about stress-response physiology (permissive effect, HPA feedback, circadian cortisol) are not addressed here.
